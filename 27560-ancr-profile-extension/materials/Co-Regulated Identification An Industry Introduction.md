# Co-Regulated Identification: An Industry Introduction, and the ANCR Extension for Digital Privacy and Consent

## Abstract

Digital identifiers, are  our online gateways, and a technological condition of interacting on the internet with devices, using commercial networks.  Traditionally identifiers and attributes are physically and naturally contained by context and reference for example, in person the identity and the scope of disclosure is apparent and inherent to context.  For example, walking into a building, signs are placed on the door. 

When online using a device, the physical  and common sense policy we operate with socially do not define the technical rules that govern  digital-id. Online, it is a privacy policy, which does not mention identifier and  the governance of its use as meta-data.  Covered under bespoke set of terms and conditions, which do not conform to the physical rules and common sense standards that define notice and consent.    No indication, transparency, or notice of disclosure. Instead governance is covered by  privatized policy. 

The Problem

In private-privacy policies, like most online based services today the individual is identified before the digital controller’s and processors are disclosed and identified.  Which is not fair.  organization is disclosed, before the purpose is stated, and before any legal basis can be checked. That sequence produces permissions rather than services governed by consent, and it leaves organizations unable to demonstrate what they disclosed and regulators unable to inspect and regulate it.

The Solution, 

Standard public privacy policy for online id governance. **co-regulated digital id**:  regulated by policy and rules operable by the public.  Not private rules and commercial technical protocols, and privately regulated, asserted and assured, by a service provider. 

Co-Regulated Digital-ID (CRDI), can be operated withauthority under two concurrent rule sets, the organisation's own rules and the public rules of treaty, law, and standards, with the public rules expressed as record structure that can be inspected before identification occurs.

The frame in which it becomes operational and works, is not a rejection of private-digital identification. It is a way to make identification governable, through four artefacts that an organization can publish without exposing personally identifiable identifiers by default, and that a regulator or an agent acting for an individual can inspect. 

Part 1, clauses 1 to 11, states the frame, including consent by default in a discovery interaction, and anonymity and inclusion, as properties of it. Part 2, clause 12, records the normative changes carried into the extension. Annex A records the open questions for working group consensus.

## Part 1. The frame

## Summary

Digital identification is now the default gate to online interaction. Identification is demanded before the accountable controller is disclosed, before purpose is stated, and before any legal basis is verifiable. That sequence produces permission, not consent, and it leaves regulators without inspectable evidence.

Co-regulated identification names the correction. Identification mechanisms operate under public rules: treaty, law, and standards, implemented as policy driven software with inspectable accountability. The objective is not to reject digital identification. The objective is to make identification governable through notice, transparency, and evidence.

For the identity industry the frame is additive rather than restrictive. It does not specify which identification technology to use, it does not require a new protocol, and it leaves the existing credential and wallet ecosystem in place. What it adds is a published accountability record, a versioned machine readable notice, a durable receipt, and an event log, so that an organisation can demonstrate what it disclosed and when, without holding more personal data than it already holds.

## 1. Identity is human managed surveillance (called sousveillance), identification is system managed surveillance 

- **Identity** is self-expression and self-identification. It is managed by the individual.
- **Identification** is the technical and organizational process a controller uses to recognise, link, or assert claims about a person.
- **Consent** is managed by humans. **Permissions** are managed by systems.

Digital consent becomes possible only when these are separated. Where they are conflated, permission fatigue is presented as consent, and surveillance by default is presented as trust.

## 2. The lacunna: the missing space between permission and consent

The gap is not a missing feature. It is a missing governed space between what the technology does and what the individual can see and control. In current practice:

- identification demands precede controller disclosure;
- purposes and legal basis arrive late or ambiguously;
- the boundary between private services and regulated authority is not inspectable;
- no durable, referenceable evidence of notice exists.

Co-regulated identification fills that lacunna with public rules and standard artefacts.

## 3. What co-regulation means, stated plainly

Co-regulation means two rule sets operate on the same identifier at once.

1. The **controller's own rules**: service terms, technical design, internal policy.
2. The **public rules**: Convention 108+ baseline, applicable law, and the standards profile that makes disclosure machine readable and inspectable.

Neither pure self-regulation nor pure state regulation governs identification well at internet scale. Self-regulation leaves the identifier privately defined. State regulation alone lacks the operational artefacts to inspect it in real time. Co-regulation supplies both: public rules expressed as record structure, and controller implementation that is verifiable before identification occurs.

## 4. The inversion: controller identification first

The operating rule is simple. **Verify who is accountable before any identification is demanded.**

1. The **Controller Identification Record (CIR)** is disclosed and resolvable before any demand for personal identification.
2. The individual, or an agent acting for the individual, can inspect who is accountable, what is claimed, what is processed, and where recourse exists.
3. Only then does any lawful interaction proceed: consent when consent is the legal basis, or contract, legal obligation, legitimate interest, vital interest, or public interest, each stated explicitly.

This makes the entrance to the controller's data silo inspectable. It supports anonymous interaction through to high assurance interaction, according to context and legal basis, without requiring identification as the price of entry.

## 5. The artefact set

Co-regulated identification produces artefacts that are inspectable, referenceable, and reusable:

| **Artefact** | **Function under co-regulation** |
| --- | --- |
| Controller Identification Record (CIR) | Public accountability anchor. Resolvable before identification is demanded. |
| Notice Record | Versioned, machine readable transparency statement. The public rule made operational. |
| Anchored Notice Receipt | Bilateral evidence that a specific notice version was disclosed, anonymous by default. |
| Notice Event Log | Append-only lifecycle record: issuance, material change, withdrawal, objection. |
| Notice Token | Portable evidence that meaningful choice occurred. Legal basis agnostic. |

Together these provide minimum notice disclosure: the smallest set of disclosures that must be present and inspectable before identification or processing begins.

## 6. Reciprocal and proportionate disclosure

Co-regulation sets a measurable standard for the disclosure itself. What the technology can do must be matched by what the individual can see and control:

- the technology class in use;
- the practical controls actually available;
- associated or derivative controls, where applicable;
- secondary purposes of use;
- any limitation or derogation on those controls.

Where the capability of the technology exceeds the controls disclosed, the disclosure is not proportionate and the identification is not co-regulated.

## 7. Consent by default in a discovery interaction

The ANCR co-regulation perspective originated in the Minimum Viable Consent Receipt (MVCR), which is itself a two factor notice specification. In that lineage, two factor notice describes the mode where the PII Principal initiates the interaction. The individual approaches the sign, the access point, the device, or the online notice, the notice is presented, and a bilateral receipt is returned. The two factors are the presentation and the evidence, counted within the notice event.

That initiation is what the operational default rests on. An individual who seeks out and reads a public notice is doing so in order to discover. Consent by default is implied operationally in that act, unless the individual is notified otherwise.

The physical analogue is the sign. Absent a sign, approach and looking are ordinarily permitted, and a private property sign is the notification that changes the default. Notice has always worked this way. The digital case introduces no new claim, it only makes the default recordable.

Three impacts.

1. **The burden sits with the controller.** Where a lawful basis other than consent applies, the controller notifies at the point of disclosure by asserting that basis in the receipt header. Silence is not an assertion of some other basis.
2. **The default is an interpretation of the notice event, not proof of legal consent for processing.** Where a secondary purpose relies on consent, that authorization is a separate receipt bound to the anchored one, carrying the notice version reference, the disclosure time, and the applicable rights context.
3. **Controller initiated disclosure is a different case.** Where the controller presents the notice and the individual has not sought it in order to discover, the operational default does not apply, and the lawful basis is stated explicitly.

Stated as a rule: the discovery act carries the default, and the notice is what displaces it.

## 8. Anonymity and inclusion

Two properties follow from the frame. They are stated here so that they are not read as exceptions granted by the controller.

**Anonymity is the default state of the evidence.** Notice, and the receipt that evidences it, do not require the individual to be identified. The Anchored Notice Receipt is generable and verifiable without an account identifier or a PII principal identifier, and retrieval of a Controller Identification Record is not itself an identification event. Identification is an outcome that some contexts and some lawful bases require. It is not a precondition for receiving notice or for holding evidence of it. Where a controller cannot produce evidence of notice without identifying the individual, the evidence has been made contingent on the very act it exists to govern.

**Inclusion means notice and recourse do not depend on possessing digital identification.** The privacy access point is specified as a set of modalities rather than a single channel, so that the accountable controller, the notice version in effect, and the route to recourse stay reachable by people who cannot, or choose not to, hold a digital identification credential. This covers accessibility needs, low or absent connectivity, delegated and assisted interaction, and in person contexts where notice and identity are physically verifiable. An implementation that offers recourse only through an authenticated digital channel excludes the people most exposed to the risk it is meant to govern.

Together these keep the frame inclusive in both senses used in this paper: inclusive of the identity management community as participants in the work, and inclusive of individuals regardless of their identification status.

## 9. Standards alignment

- **Minimum Viable Consent Receipt (MVCR):** the ANCR lineage, and the origin of the two factor notice pattern in its principal initiated mode.
- **ISO/IEC 29100:** privacy framework vocabulary and principles.
- **ISO/IEC 29184:** online notice and consent presentation expectations. Factor 1 of two factor notice.
- **ISO/IEC TS 27560:2023:** consent record information structure. The ANCR profile extends this to notice records and notice receipts. Factor 2 of two factor notice.
- **PWI 26689:** machine readable notice and ANCR exchange.
- **Convention 108+:** treaty baseline for meaningful choice and Article 8.2 notice obligations. The public rule set in the co-regulated pair.

The ANCR contribution to GitHub is best described as a community contribution providing a profile for the ISO/IEC 27560 consent record information structure, framed as transparency by default so that the consent record is not misused as a surveillance record information structure.

## 10.  Co-regulated id, modernizes data protection and regulatory capacity

1. **Inclusive positioning.** It invites the identity management community to participate rather than defend. Identification is not the problem; ungoverned identification is.
2. **Neutral standards language.** Co-regulation is describable in ISO terms without asserting a policy position on any national scheme.
3. **Legal basis agnostic.** The receipt structure serves all lawful bases, with consent as the default interpretation only where the individual initiated the interaction in order to discover and two factor notice returns a bilateral receipt.
4. **Enforceable at scale.** The artefacts are what a regulator can inspect and what a controller can publish without exposing personal data.  
5. **Cross-border interoperability.** Public rules plus standard record structure support consensual cross-border data flow with maintained authorisation.

## 11. What this asks of implementers

Nothing in this frame requires a new protocol or a change of identification technology. It asks for four things that are, and have always been required, to be published and up to date.

1. **Publish a Controller Identification Record.** Resolvable without authentication, and available before any demand for personal identification. It carries the accountable name, the jurisdiction, and at least one route to recourse that operates without a digital identification credential.
2. **Version the notice, and reference the version.** Every disclosure event binds to an immutable notice version reference, and a material change creates a new version rather than editing the previous one in place.
3. **Issue a receipt that does not require identification.** The evidence of disclosure is generable and verifiable without an account identifier or a PII principal identifier.
4. **Keep an event log.** Issuance and material change at minimum, so that a claim made about a past interaction can be checked against the notice version that was actually in effect at the time.

An organization that does these four things can demonstrate conformance per notice version, and can do so without exposing personal data to provide (Digital Privacy). An organization that cannot do them is, in practice, asking to be trusted rather than inspected. (which is not an digitally operational form of consent)

## 12. Normative changes  to the ANCR extension (Aug 2026)

The frame above is implemented in the ANCR Extension for ISO/IEC TS 27560:2023 at working revision v0.3. The following changes are applied:

1. **Introduction:** add the co-regulation statement, two rule sets on one identifier, and the identity versus identification separation.
2. **Clause 1 Scope:** state that the profile specifies the record structure through which identification is co-regulated, and that controller identification precedes personal identification.
3. **Clause 4 Terms:** add definitions for co-regulated identification, identification, identity, and minimum notice disclosure.
4. **Clause 6:** frame the artefact set as the co-regulation evidence set, with two factor notice as the mechanism.
5. **Clause 7.1:** state the CIR as the public rule anchor, resolvable before any identification demand.
6. **Clause 7.2.1:** align reciprocal and proportionate disclosure explicitly to the co-regulation test.
7. **Clause 7.2.2:** retain lawful basis interoperability, and add that co-regulation applies across all lawful bases, not only consent.
8. **Clause 3 Terms and clause 5.1:** define anonymity by default and non-exclusion, add conformance criterion C7 so that at least one recourse modality is operable without a digital identification credential, and strengthen clauses 7.1.2 and 7.3.1 accordingly.
9. **Clause 6.1:** record the MVCR lineage of two factor notice, state the principal initiated and controller initiated disclosure modes, and note that work items using one factor notice and two factor notice to denote rounds in a disclosure and authorization sequence are counting something different.
10. **Clause 7.2.2:** restate consent by default as the operational default of a discovery interaction, with the discovery rationale, the physical sign analogue, the notified otherwise mechanism as a controller obligation, an explicit statement that the default is not proof of legal consent for processing, and the exclusion of controller initiated disclosure.
